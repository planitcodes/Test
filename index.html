Reason one

You earn points on the wrong things

Most people put every dollar on one card at one point per dollar. The same spend, routed through the right categories and the right two or three products, earns three to five times as much — without spending an extra cent.

Reason two

You spend them at the worst rate

Booking through a bank's travel portal locks your points at roughly a cent each. Transferred to the right airline partner and redeemed on the right route, the same balance is routinely worth four to eight times that.

Reason three

You search after you've decided

Picking dates first and then shopping is how you pay retail. The system inverts it: watch the whole market continuously, and let the fare decide the week you travel.

The operating order

Five steps, run in this order. Skipping one is what costs people the trip.

This is a sequence, not a menu — each step depends on the one above it. Card applications before an audit burn approvals you'll want later; hunting fares before you hold transferable points means finding the seat and not being able to take it.

01

Audit what you already hold

Balances, cards, credit profile, home airports, passports.

Almost everyone is holding more usable value than they think — and at least one balance that's quietly about to expire or devalue. This is the first thing we look at on a call.

02

Build the earn stack

Match products to the spend you already have.

Groceries, rent, business expenses, tuition, insurance. We build around real, existing outflow. Manufactured spend is not part of this system and you won't be asked to do it.

03

Position for the redemption

Application order, transfer partners, timing windows.

Transfers are one-way and mostly instant-but-irreversible. Knowing which partner to hold for, and moving points only once a seat is confirmed, is where most of the value is won or lost.

04

Hunt continuously

Award space alerts plus fare errors, monitored daily.

Two separate engines run here: award seats releasing at schedule-open and at the T-14 unlock, and pricing mistakes that surface without warning and close within hours.

05

Ticket and protect it

Hold, ticket, then defend the reservation.

Booking is the start, not the end — schedule changes are the single best free upgrade lever in the business, and knowing your re-accommodation rights turns a cancelled flight into a better cabin.

Straight answers

The six things everyone asks before they book.

Will opening cards wreck my credit score?
Applications cause a small, temporary dip that typically recovers within a few months, and the added available credit usually helps utilisation over time. The real risks are missed payments and carrying a balance — interest at these rates destroys any points value instantly. If you don't pay in full every month, this system is not for you yet, and I'll tell you that on the call rather than sell you something.
Do I need to spend a lot for this to work?
No. The majority of the points in the ledger above came from signup bonuses, not from everyday spend. What matters is meeting a minimum spend on bills you already pay, in the right order and timing. Nobody is asked to buy things they don't need or to do manufactured spend.
Are mistake fares actually honoured?
Often, but not always, and honouring varies by carrier and by jurisdiction. That's why the rule is: never book non-refundable hotels or connecting tickets until the fare has ticketed and settled. I flag the risk level with every fare that gets called in the deal room, and there's a written process for what to do if an airline tries to cancel one.
I have zero points today. Is a trip in four months realistic?
In economy, usually yes. In business, sometimes — it depends on your approval odds and how flexible the dates are. Four months is tight but it's the most common situation people bring to a first call, and there's a specific plan for it that leans on transferable currencies and near-term award space rather than long slow accrual.
Does this work outside the United States?
The fare-hunting engine works everywhere. The card side is country-specific: the US has the deepest market, Canada, the UK, Australia and Singapore all have workable ecosystems, and several countries have almost nothing. Tell me your country in the form and I'll say plainly whether Engine A is viable for you before you pay for anything.
What if I've already booked the trip?
Bring it anyway. Fares and award space move constantly, many tickets can be rebooked or repriced, and schedule changes open doors that weren't there when you booked. Worst case we leave it alone and spend the call setting you up so the next one costs a fraction of this one.
