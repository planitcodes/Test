Email kit

Every message a client gets, from the playbook download through to the testimonial ask. Hit copy, paste into Gmail, replace the fields. Written to be sent as-is.

Fields to replace

  • {{FIRST}} — their first name
  • {{ROUTE}} — e.g. JFK → Tokyo
  • {{DATE}} / {{TIME}} — of the call
  • {{LINK}} — playbook, recording, plan doc, or booking link depending on the email
  • {{AMOUNT}}, {{FARE}}, {{POINTS}} — figures specific to them

Keep the subject lines. They are short and specific on purpose — long subjects get skimmed past, and clever ones get ignored.

01

Playbook delivery

Automatic · the moment they submit form 01
SubjectYour First 100,000 Points playbook

Why it reads like this. It delivers, points at the single best section, and leads with the disqualifier rather than a sales pitch. Telling someone not to buy is the fastest way to be believed later.

Why it reads like this. A prepared client is worth twice an unprepared one, and this is the only chance to ask. Pre-authorising a reschedule reduces no-shows — people who feel trapped ghost instead.

05

Two-week check-in

Day 14 · closes the follow-up window
SubjectChecking in on {{ROUTE}}
{{FIRST}} — two weeks since we spoke. Where did you get to? Specifically: did action 1 happen? That is the one everything else waits on. If you hit something and stopped, tell me what it was. Nine times out of ten it is a five-minute answer and the plan carries on. Your follow-up window technically closes today, but I am not going to be precious about it — if you are mid-application and something comes up, ask. When you do book, send me the confirmation. I keep a ledger of redemptions, names off it, and yours would sit well on it. — {{YOUR NAME}}
Why it reads like this. Asks about one specific action rather than "how's it going", which gets no reply. Softening the deadline costs nothing and buys goodwill. The ledger line plants the testimonial ask early.
06

Testimonial request

The day they tell you the trip is booked — not before
Subject{{ROUTE}} — congratulations, and one favour
{{FIRST}} — {{POINTS}} points and {{AMOUNT}} against a {{FARE}} fare. That is a genuinely good booking and you should feel smug about it. The favour: a few sentences I can put on the site. Do not write a review. Just answer whichever of these you have an opinion about, in whatever words come out: 1. What did you think this trip was going to cost, before we spoke? 2. What had you been getting wrong? 3. Was there a moment where you thought this would not work for you? 4. Who would you tell not to bother with this? Three lines is plenty. I will edit for length only, send you the exact wording before it goes up, and you can veto any of it. First name and route only — no surname, no photo, nothing else. And if you would rather not, say so and I will never mention it again. It genuinely does not affect anything. — {{YOUR NAME}}
Why it reads like this. Timed to the moment the number feels real, which is booking rather than payment. The four questions produce sentences with figures in them; "how was it?" produces "great service". Question 4 is the useful one — the objection they had is the objection your next visitor has. Approval and a clean exit are offered up front, which is both decent and what gets a yes.

Sequencing

  • 01 fires automatically from the playbook form. 02 three days later, only to people who have not booked.
  • 03 on booking, 04 within a day of the call, 05 at day fourteen.
  • 06 is not scheduled. It goes the day they tell you a trip is ticketed — which is why 04 and 05 both ask them to send the confirmation.
  • 07 only for Booked For You, always before you start searching.
  • 08 whenever it is true. It will be more often than you expect.

Until an email platform is set up, 01 and 02 are manual — watch the Sheet behind the form and send them yourself. That is fine for the first fifty people and it means you read every address that comes in.
